Group Project Team Formation

Activity playbook

A facilitation guide for using learner profile cards, private preferences, and structured invitations to form project teams.

Cohort cards Activity slide

Guiding principle

Maximize learner agency while minimizing public rejection and deadlocks.

This version adds a cohort profile webpage before team formation. Learners introduce themselves by contributing a card to the webpage, not by publicly pitching for a spot on a team.

Pre-Session: Build the Cohort Webpage

Before the live team formation session, every learner contributes one profile card to the cohort webpage.

Each learner card includes

  • Name
  • Preferred role or interests, such as frontend, backend, design, product, testing, or docs
  • Strength they can contribute
  • Skill they want to grow
  • Preferred working style
  • Availability or preferred working hours
  • What they value in a teammate
  • One project idea or product domain they are interested in

Team Leader candidates also answer

  • Why they are volunteering as Team Leader
  • How they help a team make decisions
  • How they handle conflict or blockers
  • What kind of team culture they want to create
  • What they are not trying to be responsible for, for example "not the boss"

Instructor setup

  • Create or share the cohort webpage repository
  • Provide the card template
  • Review contributions for completeness and professionalism
  • Merge cards before the live session
  • Remind learners that the webpage is for structured visibility, not public auditioning

Phase 1: Introduce the Process and Browse Cards

13:00-13:20

Step 1: Introduce the process

  • Explain why the teams are learner-formed
  • Explain that instructors will not choose teams
  • Set expectations for respect and professionalism
  • Confirm final team size is 4-5 members
  • Explain that learner cards are used to understand working style, interests, and goals
  • Clarify that the goal is not to pick friends or the most polished profile
Choose teammates based on who will help your team succeed, not simply the people you already know best.

Step 2: Browse the cohort webpage

Everyone reviews the profile cards. Focus on:

  • Interests and project domains
  • Complementary strengths
  • Availability and working style
  • Learning goals
  • Team Leader candidate answers

This replaces public learner pitches. Learners have visibility without being forced to sell themselves in front of the room.

Phase 2: Elect 10 Team Leaders

13:20-13:40

If more than 10 learners declare Team Leader interest

  • Everyone reviews the Team Leader specific card answers
  • Each candidate may give a short 30-second clarification
  • Everyone votes anonymously
  • The top 10 become Team Leaders
  • Ties are resolved randomly

If exactly 10 learners declare Team Leader interest

They automatically become Team Leaders.

If fewer than 10 learners declare Team Leader interest

  • Reopen nominations
  • Newly nominated learners update or confirm their Team Leader card answers

Phase 3: Gather Preferences

13:40-13:55

Every non-Team Leader submits a private form.

Include

  • 1st preferred Team Leader
  • 2nd preferred Team Leader
  • 3rd preferred Team Leader

The cohort webpage is visible while learners submit preferences.

Optional but valuable

  • Frontend / backend / design interest
  • Preferred working hours
  • Biggest strength
  • Biggest learning goal

Phase 4: Team Formation

13:55-15:20

Instead of continuous picking, use three structured rounds.

The cohort webpage remains available during each round so teams can consider interests, strengths, working style, and availability.

Round 1

Every Team Leader privately invites one learner.

  • Should invite someone who selected them
  • Should use the learner card for fit, not popularity
  • If multiple invitations occur, the learner chooses privately

Now every team has 2 people.

Round 2

The two-person team discusses and invites one learner.

  • Unanimous agreement preferred
  • Otherwise majority vote, where both people must agree
  • If disagreement remains after 5 minutes, use a random draw between the shortlisted candidates

Now every team has 3 people.

Round 3

The three-person team invites one learner by majority vote.

Now every team has 4 people.

Round 4, only if needed

Some teams receive a fifth member. Determine beforehand how many teams will have five members, randomly choose those teams, then repeat the same invitation process.

Phase 5: Completion Round

15:20-15:35

Handle declined invitations, remaining learners, and incomplete teams.

  • Remaining learners rank remaining teams
  • Remaining teams rank interested learners
  • Continue matching until everyone belongs to a team

Phase 6: Break

15 minutes.

Phase 7: First Team Meeting

Prioritise operational alignment instead of spending lots of time on logos.

Introductions

  • Background
  • Strengths
  • Areas they want to improve
  • Availability
  • Anything from their card that teammates should know

Team charter

  • Communication platform
  • Meeting schedule
  • Response expectations
  • Coding standards
  • Git workflow
  • PR expectations
  • Conflict resolution
  • Decision-making method

Roles

Choose initial roles such as Scrum Master, Repository owner, Product Owner, or UI lead. State that these may rotate.

Business brainstorm

  • Generate five ecommerce ideas
  • Narrow to two
  • Assign overnight research

Responsibilities

Team Leaders

  • Organise discussions
  • Keep meetings moving
  • Encourage participation
  • Help the team make decisions
  • Are not "the boss"

Members

  • Contribute
  • Review PRs
  • Communicate blockers
  • Attend meetings
  • Support teammates

Selection criteria

  • Reliability
  • Communication
  • Willingness to learn
  • Complementary skills
  • Availability
  • Attitude
